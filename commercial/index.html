Restaurant Services

Restaurant and Food Service HVAC

Few buildings work an HVAC system harder than a restaurant. Every burner, fryer, and oven adds heat, the exhaust hoods draw conditioned air out nonstop, and the makeup air unit has to replace that air without upsetting the building's pressure. Tinley Park HVAC sizes, repairs, and maintains the rooftop units, split systems, and makeup air equipment that keep both the kitchen and the dining room comfortable, and we schedule visits around your hours.

Many restaurant comfort complaints trace back to air balance. If a kitchen runs negative, it pulls dining-room air the wrong way, drags hot or cold outdoor air through every door, and wears out your staff during the rush. Our technicians balance the makeup air to the hood exhaust and log every visit in written reports that landlords and auditors can review.

Your schedule comes first. Our team coordinates every replacement and maintenance visit with owners, managers, and kitchen staff. When the work can't happen during service, we install after hours, and we keep disruption to a minimum so the kitchen keeps running and the dining room stays comfortable.

Safe Installation

Safe Installations, Documented Service

Badly installed or neglected HVAC equipment can lead to carbon monoxide hazards, poor indoor air quality, refrigerant leaks, and costly water damage from clogged condensate lines. Those are real risks for a commercial property owner responsible for employees, customers, and tenants. Our commercial installations and inspections cover heat exchanger integrity, venting and combustion air, electrical connections and disconnects, refrigerant charge, condensate drainage, and ventilation rates for occupied spaces, and each visit ends with a written report listing risks, priorities, and recommended preventive steps.

The paperwork matters as much as the repair. We keep a service binder with detailed service logs and temperature and filter records, so you can demonstrate due diligence to landlords, insurers, and auditors. Heavy-use equipment, in kitchens, gyms, and large mechanical rooms, gets a more frequent visit schedule.
