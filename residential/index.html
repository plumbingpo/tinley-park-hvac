Clear Pricing

Clear Pricing Before Any Work Starts

Before we touch a tool, we figure out exactly what's wrong. Our technicians check your system's age and tonnage, the refrigerant charge, the heat exchanger and coils, and the condition of your ductwork. Then you get an honest recommendation and the full price up front, whether the job is swapping a weak capacitor or replacing the whole system. No hidden fees, no last-minute add-ons.

Many HVAC problems stay hidden until a failure is well underway. Refrigerant seeps out through pinhole leaks in the evaporator coil, blower bearings wear quietly, and a weakening capacitor gives no warning until the compressor won't start on the first hot day. If you notice weak airflow, rising energy bills, ice on the refrigerant lines, or rooms that never reach the thermostat setting, schedule an inspection sooner rather than later. An early diagnosis usually keeps a repairable issue from becoming a replacement.

Do you know how old your system is? When a furnace or AC gets past the 12-year mark, it's worth planning the next one before the current one decides for you. Emergency replacements happen on the worst days, in heat waves and cold snaps, when choices are limited and everyone is uncomfortable. Our team knows how the Tinley Park climate affects equipment and will tell you plainly whether yours has years left or is nearing the end.

Preventive Care

Tune-Ups That Keep Your System Running

Each maintenance visit from Tinley Park HVAC is more than a filter change. Our technician cleans the coils, verifies the refrigerant charge, tests capacitors and contactors, inspects the burners and heat exchanger, clears the condensate drain, replaces the filter, and measures static pressure across the ducts. Minor faults get caught while they're inexpensive to correct, and the equipment stays running clean and efficient.

Every maintenance plan we write is a little different. A furnace in its later years needs a closer look than a new heat pump, and a dusty area means condenser coils clog faster. We build spring cooling and fall heating visits around your equipment and climate, so every tune-up keeps the system efficient and extends the life of the equipment you've already paid for.

Consider maintenance as a safeguard for equipment you've already paid for. Each tune-up keeps the coils clean, the charge correct, and the filter fresh, which lowers what you pay to run the system while easing the strain on the compressor and blower. For most families, the plan costs far less than an early replacement.

Right-Sized Systems

The Right Size System for Your Home

Getting the size right is one of the most overlooked parts of any install. Before recommending new equipment, Tinley Park HVAC works through the details that drive your home's heating and cooling load: square footage, insulation, window exposure, and duct layout. The result is a system that runs in long, efficient cycles, keeps humidity in check, and holds every room at the temperature you set.

Houses change over time. A finished basement, an addition, or two new bedrooms can outgrow the single-stage system that used to be plenty. Today's two-stage and variable-speed equipment holds tighter temperatures and uses far less energy. Whether you're comparing a heat pump and a furnace-and-AC pair, or ducted and ductless, we'll explain the honest pros and cons in SEER2 ratings, operating cost, electrical and venting needs, and installation work.

Frequent Failures

Common HVAC Problems We Fix in Tinley Park

No cooling, a furnace that refuses to light, a frozen evaporator coil, leaking refrigerant: each of these problems has its own cause and needs a different repair. Tinley Park HVAC tracks down the exact fault before replacing anything, whether it's a condenser that hums but won't start, a furnace that short-cycles, a thermostat that keeps losing its program, a clogged condensate drain, or a mini-split flashing error codes.

Some problems are mostly a nuisance, while others pose real risks. A weak capacitor simply keeps the compressor from starting, but a cracked heat exchanger can leak combustion gases into your living space, and a blocked condensate drain can quietly ruin a ceiling. We match the fix to the failure: capacitors, contactors, and fan motors on cooling systems, igniters, flame sensors, and control boards on furnaces, sealed and repaired ductwork where conditioned air is bleeding into the attic, and refrigerant recovered and recharged after a repaired leak. Whatever you're facing, our team has the training and parts to handle it.
